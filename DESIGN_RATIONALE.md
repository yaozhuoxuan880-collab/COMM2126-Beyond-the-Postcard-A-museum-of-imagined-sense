# Research-led interaction map (for the creator's academic reflection)

**Research question**

> How do promotional travel photographs shape our imagined sensory experiences of places, and how might photographs taken by visitors challenge these expectations?

This file is a **design rationale**, not a claim of research findings. It explains how creative decisions connect to the three primary assigned readings, and identifies possible limitations to evaluate after user testing. The visitor-facing museum deliberately does not name the theorists in questions.

## Primary readings — priority

- **Degen & Rose (2012), *The Sensory Experiencing of Urban Design***: Sensory experiences of environments are mediated by bodily movement and perceptual memory. This is a study of lived urban encounters, not evidence that travel photos have any particular effect. Our activities *extend* its concepts to the imaginary encounters the photographs invite.
- **Kate McLean, *Sensory Maps* (2019 final proof; verify assigned edition)**: Sensed qualities and subjective, temporally situated perspectives can be expressed through different forms of mapping. All our maps are *imagined*, not empirical sensory records.
- **D'Ignazio & Klein (2020), *Data Feminism*, ch. 4 “What Gets Counted Counts”**: Categories are not neutral and may render experiences difficult to express or invisible. Our selectors include free-text or uncertainty options; nevertheless sliders and fields still simplify experiences.

**Supplementary contextual only**: Urry & Larsen (2011), *The Tourist Gaze 3.0*; Stepchenkova & Zhan (2013), comparative analysis of promotional and user-generated destination photographs. They frame *why these kinds of photographs are compared*; they are not the main sensory frameworks.

## Museum-wide framing

- **Prologue:** invites imagination without prescribing the “correct” conclusion. Opening confidence 1–5.
- **Before / after:** separately stores what the person imagines under promotional and visitor photographs. No assertion that the visitor photo is reality or that changes are caused *solely* by the source of the picture.
- **Sensory Collection:** five creator-made postcards, showing change or no change.
- **Become the Curator:** selecting an image and text may influence someone else's sensory imagination. A participant reflects on their own editorial role, rather than concluding that all promotional photographs are false.
- **Final Sensory Receipt at reception:** preserves the original souvenir-like museum exit; reflects on what remains beyond the frame; browser print/save as PDF.

## Evidence limits to document honestly

1. User-generated/visitor images and promotional images differ in camera position, time, lighting, season and content; this is **not a controlled causal experiment**.
2. The UK photos represent different vantage points within the Lake District; do not label them a matched Buttermere shoot.
3. The Iceland photos differ markedly in time and lighting; don't infer a specific person's body temperature or comfort.
4. All sensory experiences are visitor **imaginations**, not actual recordings, biometric measurements or verified in-situ conditions.
5. Fixed slider values and sound/weather choices may shape what participants say even with 'not sure' / other choices. Record user feedback and actual revisions before writing the reflection.
6. The website selection and the order 'promotional first, visitor second' are themselves a curatorial intervention worth critically examining.
7. Public publication metadata/permissions and course AI-use declaration should be checked separately before final submission.
